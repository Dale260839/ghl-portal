## What shipped

**The Hub reads `deals` for the first time.** It only ever read `projects`, which
is the far end of the pipeline, so everything upstream — who applied, who was
matched, whether anything was signed — was invisible. That invisibility is what
hid the finding.

**`/dashboard/pipeline`** makes it visible to the people who can act on it. The
data chose its design: I measured the age spread before picking a threshold, and
20 of 23 deals were already past a month. A binary "stalled" flag at any value
lights up 87% of the board, and a screen where almost everything is highlighted
has highlighted nothing. So: bands, not a flag.

Two things that screen must not imply, both fixed structurally rather than in
copy. It is **occupancy, not flow** — `status` says where a deal is now, and a
deal that advances leaves the count it came from, so it is titled "Where deals
are now". And **signed is not a stage** — it cuts across `status`, so putting it
in the bars would double-count; a test pins that the bars still sum to the total.

**The question open since 2026-08-20 is answered.** How a bid is classified as
won is not on `projects` — it is `signature_signed_at` or `sent_to_crm_at` on the
deal. The definition lives in one function, because two columns can each mean won
and they do not arrive together, and spreading that judgement across screens is
how two of them start disagreeing.

**The signed-only filter exists and ships off.** I proved why rather than
asserting it: with the flag on, Alliance goes from 9 projects to 1, and the
survivor only appears because it has no deal to judge.

**The handoff path is built, and the whole loop rehearses.** Nine steps —
signature, Send-to-CRM, GoHighLevel, routing, WF1, the contractor seeing it, a
crew update, the PM publishing, the homeowner seeing it. 27 effects across three
workflows. Pure: no network, no clock, no writes, and running it twice is
asserted identical. `npm run rehearse`.

It proves the negative too, which matters more. `Approved Internally` is **not**
approved for the client, and the rehearsal checks the gate in both directions
plus refuses a contact associated with a different project. Turning the portal
master switch off makes the chain report broken — so the privacy block is
load-bearing rather than decorative.

## Three things building it found

**WF1 had no trigger at all.** The router listed WF2 through WF8. A signed deal
would have arrived, been verified, routed nowhere and done nothing. Fixed.

**The shared key rejects every project in the database.** The contract requires
`BSP-YYYY-NNNNNN`; `projects.id` is a UUID (101/101), `project_code` is `BSA-NNN`
(48/101), `award_code` is empty. I did not resolve it — the key is an open
decision and picking one is the reading error I made twice last week. The mapping
fails precisely instead, naming the field and who owns it.

Worth noting: D4 names the format `APS-081` and BuildSuite holds `BSA-002`. Same
shape, different prefix. `project_code` is very likely what was meant, and nobody
had looked at that column.

**A budget band is not a contract amount.** `contract_amount` must be a number;
BuildSuite has `budget_range`, a band, in two different vocabularies in the same
column. Taking one end of a band puts a wrong number on a contract.

## The demo toggle is gone, and what that cost

Removed on instruction — the switch, the cookie, the server action, and the
fixture tenant-scope override it also carried. Not hidden behind an environment
variable: dead code behind a flag is one variable away from returning.

**It cost the walkthrough its spine, and that should be said plainly.** The toggle
existed because the loop the product is *for* has no data on the real database.
Measured today: 9 real projects, contract value and outstanding balance both a
dash, review queue empty, nothing published, and the client portal rendering its
empty state on every screen.

So steps 9–19 of the client walkthrough can be described but not demonstrated.
The rules behind them are real and tested; there is nothing on screen to point at.

**One task fixes it** — Sing running `0001_hub_tables.sql`, nine tables,
create-only. It is item 1 on his page.

The HUD had to be repaired rather than updated, because removing the toggle
falsified it: it told a presenter to point at an amber "Demo data" banner and say
"this screen is on demo data". Rescraping the real screens found five more steps
pointing at content that no longer exists. All corrected, and the client-portal
steps now say plainly that there is nothing to click and what to say instead.
