## The headline: onboarding a contractor no longer involves us

Until yesterday, adding a contractor meant someone going into their GoHighLevel
sub-account, creating an API key by hand, copying it into the app's settings and
redeploying. Four steps, one of them handling a live credential, and forgetting
any of them meant that contractor's invoices failed silently.

That is gone. The app is now installed across the agency, and **a contractor
opens Project Hub from their own menu and is signed in as themselves, with
invoices and emails working, having done nothing.** New sub-accounts are covered
automatically.

It took two false starts to get there, both because of GoHighLevel's own rules:
scopes we need are only issued to sub-account installs, and a published app's
pricing cannot be changed. The version that works is simpler than the one that
did not.

## What changed for each of the three people who use it

### The homeowner can now answer an update

Publishing an update used to be one-way. They read it and that was the end of
it — no way to say "got it", no way to ask a question without picking up the
phone, and **no way for the contractor to know it had been read at all.**

Now each published update has a **Got it** button and an **Ask a question** box.
One tap for the common case, typing only when they have something to say.

### The contractor can see who read what, and reply

On the Field Updates screen, every published update now shows whether the
homeowner marked it as read, and anything they asked. The contractor can reply
either **to the homeowner** or **to the file** — one tick box, and it is
unticked by default. A note that should have been sent can be sent a minute
later; a note the homeowner should never have read cannot be unread.

### The crew can see their schedule

A contractor could already tag a crew member to an appointment and email them
about it. The crew member had **nowhere in the app to see it** — the email was
the whole of their access to their own schedule, so if it was never sent or they
deleted it, the appointment may as well not have existed.

They now have a Schedule tab, and the next few appointments appear on their
Today screen, which is the screen someone opens to find out where they are meant
to be.

### "Tasks" in the contractor's menu now opens tasks

It opened **Active work** — a different screen about a different thing — so
anyone looking for the tasks they had assigned concluded the feature did not
exist. It was there, one level down inside each project. There is now a list
across every project: what was handed out, whether the crew member has opened
it, and what is waiting on review.

### A sign-in we cannot place says so, politely

Some sign-ins are not yet linked to a contractor record in BuildSuite. They used
to be shown the shell branded as **another contractor's company**, which is worse
than showing nothing. That now reads as a plain explanation of what needs fixing
and who fixes it, with no company name attached to it.

## Two answers you were owed

**Milestone statuses.** You were asked in September to name the values and it
stayed open on our list. It turns out the question was answered in the code on
14 September and the list never caught up. The vocabulary in use is **Not
Started · In Progress · Completed · Blocked**, and anything else is now refused
rather than stored. *If you want **Delayed** as a fifth, say so and it is a
ten-minute change.*

**"51 projects have no project code, so those homeowners can never sign in."**
That has been on the worry list since 10 September. It is not true, and we
measured rather than assumed: of 115 projects, 53 have no code — and **every one
of them is a draft, active or completed project, none of them awarded. All nine
awarded projects have both a code and a client email.** Codes are assigned at
award, exactly as suspected. Nobody is locked out, and nothing is waiting on
Sing for this.

## What still needs a decision from you

1. **The signed-contract link is public.** Anyone holding the URL can open the
   signed contract without signing in. It has been that way since the feature
   shipped. Do you want it behind a login, behind an expiring link, or left as
   it is?

2. **The homeowner sign-in.** You raised a concern about this on 8 September and
   it was never answered — meanwhile the flow shipped: a homeowner signs in with
   their email and their project code, typed on a phone. **Our recommendation is
   to replace it with a link emailed to them** — they tap it and they are in. It
   removes the "I can't get in" call entirely, and it is more secure than a code
   that is printed on documents and never expires. A day's work, and it would
   close your open question.

3. **Delayed milestones** — see above.

4. **A payment gateway in GoHighLevel.** Invoices can be created and sent today;
   nothing can actually be *paid* until a gateway is connected. That is an
   account-level setting, not something we can do.

---

## Known and not fixed

- **A contractor is a sub-account, not a person.** Everyone on a contractor's
  staff who opens the menu link shares one login, so nothing is attributable to
  an individual. Fixing it properly means running Project Hub inside
  GoHighLevel as an embedded page. Worth doing; not started.
- **GoHighLevel events never reach us.** We have never been given the webhook
  secret, so the automations that are meant to fire on events in GoHighLevel
  cannot be triggered by it at all.
