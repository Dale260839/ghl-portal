# Project Hub — the photographs arrive

**25 September 2026** · written to be sent to Chris
**Live at:** https://project-hub-one-vert.vercel.app

---

## What a homeowner saw yesterday

A grey box with the word **"Photo"** in it. Never the photograph.

And which photos appeared at all was arbitrary: the app took the two most recent
pictures on the whole project and attached them to the newest update. On a real
job that means every update showed either somebody else's photographs or none.

For a homeowner watching their house being rebuilt, the photographs are the
product. They were the one thing that never arrived.

## What changed

**A photograph now belongs to the update it was sent with.** When a crew member
submits their daily update with photos, those photos are filed against that
update — not guessed at afterwards. The homeowner's feed shows the actual
pictures, tappable to full size.

**The project manager sees what they are approving.** Until today a PM read the
crew's words on one screen while the pictures sat on another, and published
without them. The photographs are now on the update being reviewed, each with
its own switch.

**Each photograph is released on its own, and none is released by default.** A
crew member photographs what they need to remember — a neighbour's car, a mess
mid-job, somebody's arm — which is not the same as what a homeowner should see.
The PM ticks the ones that should go, one at a time.

**The PM's notification email told the truth for the first time.** Every
submission said "0 photos", including the ones with six. It was hardcoded.

## What this needs from you

**One thing, and everything above is waiting on it.**

Run `supabase/hub/0019_photo_update_link.sql` on the Hub database
(`nexpqqxarimqmntnvzff` → SQL Editor → paste → Run). Expect *Success. No rows
returned.*

It adds one nullable column to `hub_photos`. Nothing is rewritten, it is safe to
run at any time, and re-running it does nothing.

**Until it is run**, photos are saved exactly as they were yesterday and an
update shows its text with no photographs — which is honest, and better than the
wrong ones. **After it is run**, only *new* submissions are linked: photographs
already uploaded have no update to point at, and guessing one would recreate the
bug this removes. Older updates keep their text; their photos stay on the
project's Photos page.

**Then check it, which takes five minutes:**

1. On a test project, submit a field update with two photos.
2. On Field Updates, confirm both appear under that update, marked **Internal**.
3. Tick one. Publish the update.
4. Open the homeowner's portal: the released photograph is there and opens full
   size; the other is not, and its link does not work even for somebody holding
   it.

If step 4 shows both, stop and tell me — that is the only failure here that
matters.

## For us — the operational half

Not for sending.

**Shipped:** 1 commit · 11 files · 1,198 tests green, typecheck and build clean,
both remotes level at `f0a67b4`.

**Migration 0019** — `hub_photos.update_id`, nullable, indexed,
`on delete set null`. Same shape and the same §3.6 argument as 0015. **Not run.**

**The two-halves write path.** The uploader posts each photo as it is taken, so
rows exist before the update does. `storePhoto` now returns the created id, the
uploader renders one hidden `photoId` input per *saved* photo, and
`submitFieldUpdate` links them after `createUpdate`. A failed upload contributes
no id. A failure to link is logged and never loses the update.

**Where the privacy rule lives.** `listForUpdates(scope, kind, ids,
clientVisibleOnly)` — the flag is a required positional argument, not an option
with a default, and `UpdatePhotos` never reads `clientVisible` at all. A
component that filtered its own props would put the rule in a template.

**Break tests, four, all caught:** unreleased photos reaching the portal;
arbitrary project photos pinned to an update again; the grey box returning; the
release rule migrating into the template.

**Left alone deliberately:** photos uploaded before 0019 stay unlinked. A
backfill would have to guess by timestamp, which is the bug being removed.
