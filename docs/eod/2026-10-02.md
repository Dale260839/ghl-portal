## 1 · Two screens worked at twenty records and failed at four hundred

**The homeowner's photo gallery** was one long grid, newest first, with no dates
between the pictures. That reads perfectly well at twenty photographs.

A single kitchen produces four hundred. At that size a homeowner looking for
"the week the units went in" has no way to find it — the grid is a wall, and
scrolling it tells them nothing about when anything happened.

It is grouped by month now, newest first, each month carrying a count and
staying on screen while you scroll through it. Months are how people remember a
renovation.

**The crew's messages** merged every job into one list. At two projects that
reads as a conversation. At ten it is four people talking about four different
houses in one stream, and a crew member cannot follow any of them. One project
at a time now, chosen from a row across the top, and the reply box follows
whichever thread is open — sending a message to the wrong job is a mistake
nobody means to make.

## 2 · A page that says whether the system is actually working

Twice this week something broke and **we found out because a person told us**:
the database outage arrived as an email from Sing, and the operator buttons
showing to every contractor arrived because Dale looked at a screenshot.

Nothing in the product said "I cannot reach the database" until somebody could
not sign in.

There is now a **Health** page, visible only to the agency's own account. Every
check on it is something that has actually gone wrong in the last ten days: a
key that stopped working, migrations written and never run, email silently
switched off, operator controls available to everybody.

Two things it deliberately will not do. It shows **no project, no client and no
figures** — a status page that lists a contractor's jobs is a back door with a
friendly name. And it never shows a key, only whether one is set.

There is a matching address a monitoring service can watch, which returns an
error code when something is broken, so in future this can page somebody rather
than wait to be noticed.

## 3 · Tidying that was overdue

Four screens still carried sample data left over from before the database
existed. None of it was being shown — but that is exactly how last week's
dashboard ended up computing real figures from invented change orders. It is
gone, and the build now fails if sample data finds its way onto any screen at
all.

**The blockers list has been reconciled with reality**: five items were still
listed as open that had been resolved for days, two of them marked as urgent.
Four genuinely open items were added. A list nobody trusts is worse than no list.

## 4 · What is *not* done, honestly

- **Warranty**, on the Completion screen. There is no warranty data in the
  system and inventing some would be worse than the gap. It needs a decision
  about what you actually promise, and for how long.
- **Read receipts on messages.** A project manager still cannot tell whether a
  crew member has seen one — the same gap we closed for homeowners last week.
- **Single sign-on**, which is the answer to two separate problems: a contractor
  is still a *company* rather than a named person, so nothing is attributable to
  an individual, and sign-in links from GoHighLevel still cannot be signed.

## 5 · The four things waiting on you — step by step

### A · Run migration `0019` — 3 minutes

1. Supabase → the **Project Hub** database (`nexpqqxarimqmntnvzff` — *not* the
   BuildSuite one) → **SQL Editor** → **New query**.
2. Open `supabase/hub/0019_photo_update_link.sql` and paste the whole file.
3. **Run.** Expect *Success. No rows returned.*

Until this runs, photographs cannot belong to the update they were sent with, so
the homeowner sees an update's words with no pictures. Safe at any time, safe to
re-run.

### B · Set `ADMIN_LOCATION_IDS` — 2 minutes

1. Vercel → **project-hub** → **Settings → Environment Variables** → **Add**.
2. Name `ADMIN_LOCATION_IDS`, value `IifYfP2B2NUaoDPdsTTa` (Alliance For
   Contractors). Production.
3. **Deployments → ⋯ → Redeploy.** Variables only take effect on a deploy.

Until this is set **nobody** has "Switch account" or "Viewing as" — including
you. Empty meaning nobody is deliberate: a default that guesses who the
administrator is will eventually guess in someone else's favour.

### C · Check the new Health page — 1 minute

Open **Health** in the sidebar (it only appears once step B is done).

Expect to see `migration-0019` go from *NOT RUN* to *applied* after step A, and
`operator-controls` turn green after step B. Anything showing **Broken** is
worth a message to me; anything showing **Check** is a decision rather than a
fault.

## For us — the operational half

Not for sending.

**Shipped:** 2 commits · 15 files, +863 / −43 · 1,235 tests green (+16),
typecheck and build clean, both remotes level at `e4ab3d5`.

**New:** `lib/photo-groups.ts` (+6 tests), `lib/health.ts` (+8 tests),
`/dashboard/health`, `/api/health`.

**The health report's two hard rules**, both guardrailed: it may not reach for
`listProjects`, `clientName`, `projectName` or `listDailyUpdates`; and no secret
appears in the output — a test feeds it six real-looking secrets and asserts
none of them is in the JSON. It answers rather than throwing when the databases
are unreachable, which is the case it exists for, and migrations are detected by
the **column they add** rather than by a number somebody has to maintain.

`/api/health` takes an operator session or `CRON_SECRET` compared in full, never
by prefix. Unauthorised gets **404**, not 401 — a 401 confirms the route exists.
503 when any check fails, so a monitor needs no parser.

**Six deliberate breaks, all caught.** One needed re-breaking: the first attempt
missed its anchor and reported nothing rather than passing — the right failure
mode, still a miss.

**Guardrail widened:** fixture imports are now banned from every screen, not
only `app/field` and `app/portal`.

**Still ours, unblocked:** delete the PITs once contractors are proven on the
Marketplace path; read state on messages (needs a migration); the warranty half
of Completion (needs a product decision, see §4).
