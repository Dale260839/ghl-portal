# Project Hub — five things only a phone would have shown us

**9 October 2026**
**Live at:** https://project-hub-one-vert.vercel.app

> Every fix below came from a screenshot Dale took on a phone. None of them was
> caught by 1,473 passing tests, a typecheck or a production build, because
> none of those things look at anything.

---

## 1 · The header pushed the page sideways

On a phone the account switcher, the view switcher, the user name and Sign out
came to roughly 400px in a bar 320px wide — and the row they sat in was marked
as unable to shrink. So instead of the controls giving, the page did: the brand
was shoved out of the bar and the whole screen gained a horizontal scroll.

It gives now, and the controls inside it shed their labels rather than the
layout shedding the controls.

**That distinction is the whole fix.** The obvious answer is to hide the
operator controls on small screens. But mid-view the session reads as somebody
else, and the view switcher is the way back — hiding it strands an admin who
switched view at their desk and then opened the Hub on the way to a site. So
they shrink to their icon instead, and each carries a spoken label, because a
button with no text needs a name.

## 2 · The menus opened off the side of the screen

Both dropdowns hung off the left edge, half of each menu unreachable.

**My first fix this morning was wrong**, and the second screenshot is what
showed it. I capped the menus to the width of the screen, which does nothing:
a menu is anchored to the **right edge of the button that opened it** and grows
leftward. The account switcher sits in the middle of the header, so its menu
began about ninety pixels off the left of the screen — and would have at any
width worth opening.

On a phone the menus now hang from the **header** instead of from the button,
spanning it with a small gutter either side. On anything bigger, nothing
changed.

## 3 · The crew's task cards

Three problems on one card, with three different causes.

**The status pills wrapped.** "Ready for Review" became three lines inside a
round pill and read as a smudge. The pill was being squeezed by the task title
beside it; it is now the title that gives and the pill that does not. Fixed in
the one component every crew status goes through, because a pill that wraps is
broken everywhere it appears.

**The buttons wrapped too**, and after a submit the confirmation message
appeared as a *third column* beside them, squeezing all three and doubling the
height of the row. The buttons have their own row now, 48px tall rather than
44, because this is the control used standing on a ladder. The message sits
underneath, where a sentence belongs.

**And one button did nothing.** A task already In Progress still offered
"Start", and tapping it returned "Already In Progress." A control whose only
purpose is to report that it did nothing should not be a control. The action
matching the task's current state is disabled and says what it is — *Started*,
*Submitted*.

## 4 · The two-to-three second delay on every crew tap

This one was not a slow query. The reads were already running in parallel.

There was **no loading screen anywhere under the crew section** — ten screens,
no boundary. Two things followed, and only the first is obvious:

- Nothing to show between the tap and the server's answer, so the old screen
  sat there looking ignored, and got tapped again.
- Less obviously: the browser only pre-loads a page *as far as its loading
  screen*. With none, the pre-loading the framework was already doing bought
  nothing at all, and every tap paid the full round trip on the spot.

One loading screen now covers all ten, shaped like the real screens so content
lands into the same layout instead of shoving it. The bottom bar also responds
to the tap immediately — the destination lights up and pulses, the way the
contractor sidebar has since September.

**The data still takes the same time to arrive.** What changed is that the app
answers instantly and says it is working.

## 5 · The project dropdowns ran off the screen

The control was not the problem and could not have been. A dropdown list is
drawn by the browser, not by us — it cannot be positioned or clipped, and it
sizes itself to its longest entry. "General Remodeling / Additions project in
Bellevue (+1 more)" is fifty-nine characters.

So the names are shortened. **From the middle, not the end**, because these
names end in the part that tells them apart: cutting the tail turns
"…project in Bellevue" and "…project in Redmond" into the same line, and a crew
member files their day's work against the wrong house. If shortening ever does
merge two different names, both go back to full length — a wide dropdown is a
nuisance, two jobs a crew member cannot tell apart is a wrong house.

## What today actually says

Five defects, on the surface used by the people who are outdoors with gloves
on, and the test suite was green through all of them. Tests hold behaviour.
They do not look at the screen, and nothing in this product had.

That gap is still open: **none of this week's interface work has been seen in a
browser by me.** Today's five were found because Dale looked. The remaining
ones will be found the same way.
